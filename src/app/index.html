Ballot Gap Lab

How hard is it for Brazil's real ballot-transmission process to produce the near-straight decline in the Flávio − Lula gap seen between 18:15 and 20:15 (Brasília) on 4 Oct 2026? This page does not assume the count was clean or rigged. It measures the shape.

Why a straight line is a non-trivial shape

The national gap is a cumulative ratio: G(t) = (F(t) − L(t)) / V(t). If a batch of ΔV valid votes arrives with margin m (Flávio − Lula, in points), then Gnew = (G·V + m·ΔV) / (V + ΔV), i.e. dG/dt = (m − G) · V′(t) / V(t).

With a fixed incoming mix m and a steady flow V′, the slope shrinks like 1/V: each new batch is a smaller share of a bigger pile, so the gap curve bends (a hyperbola, flattening over time). A constant slope b needs m(t) = G(t) + b · V(t) / V′(t): the incoming Lula advantage has to grow as the pile grows, or the arrival rate V′ has to rise in step with V. The models below test whether real arrival order (which part of the country reports when) does that on its own.
